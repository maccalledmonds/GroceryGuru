A

One bold accent color used sparingly

Everything stays near-black and white — except one element (the primary button, active ingredient tags, or a single highlight line) gets a vivid, unexpected color. Think deep amber, forest green, or a warm coral. The contrast makes it feel intentional, not decorated.

B

Editorial-weight typography

Use a high-contrast type pairing: a condensed or display-weight serif for section headers ("Cook Now", "Almost There") paired with a clean sans for body text. Creates a food-magazine feel while staying minimal — the kind of typographic detail that immediately signals "designer made this."

C

Distinctive ingredient tag design

The ingredient chips are the most-touched UI element. Instead of generic pill badges, design them with real character: square corners + dashed border, or a bold outlined style, or a subtle hand-drawn-feeling irregular border radius. Users interact with these constantly — making them beautiful pays off.

D

Structural layout personality

Add one layout-level detail that feels intentional: a thick left-border accent on recipe cards, a bold horizontal rule with the section title inline, or a numbered index column beside the recipe list. These are the details that designers use to create systems that feel coherent rather than assembled.
